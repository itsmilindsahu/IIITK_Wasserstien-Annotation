\begin{abstract}
Video summarization aims to distill long video streams into concise, representative summaries. A fundamental yet often neglected challenge in supervised video summarization is \emph{multi-annotator disagreement}: independent human annotators exhibit divergent, multimodal preferences regarding what constitutes important video segments. Existing diffusion-based video summarization frameworks (e.g., Shang et al., AAAI-25) collapse multiple human annotations into an arithmetic mean, fundamentally violating the geometry of human disagreement and generating unrepresentative targets that lie off the probability simplex $\Delta^{N-1}$. 

In this work, we propose a principled geometric framework for \textbf{Multi-annotator Simplex-Preserving Diffusion Models (MSPDM)}. Our framework introduces three core contributions: 
(1) \textbf{Wasserstein-2 Barycenter Consensus}: Rather than Euclidean averaging, we compute the target summary via the exact 1D Wasserstein-2 barycenter using quantile-function averaging, provably preserving multimodal annotation peaks;
(2) \textbf{Simplex-Preserving Dirichlet Forward Process}: We replace unconstrained Gaussian diffusion with a Dirichlet forward process coupled with an entropic simplex projection $\text{Proj}_\Delta$, guaranteeing that intermediate states $x_t$ remain strictly on the probability simplex $\Delta^{N-1}$;
(3) \textbf{Differentiable Cramér-Regularized Objective}: We train the noise predictor using a combined MSE and exact closed-form Cramér distance loss, penalizing cumulative distribution discrepancies under Optimal Transport principles. 

Furthermore, we address critical methodological pitfalls in prior evaluations by establishing a \textbf{leak-free evaluation protocol}: annotators per video are partitioned into a barycenter-construction set ($K_{\text{train}}=15$) and a held-out set ($K_{\text{heldout}}=5$), and evaluation is performed on samples generated from the full reverse diffusion process against held-out annotators using the true 1D Wasserstein distance ($W_2$). Benchmarking across all 50 videos of the TVSum dataset demonstrates that our framework achieves statistically significant improvements over the Euclidean baseline ($p = 0.0302$ on paired $t$-test, $p = 0.0400$ on Wilcoxon signed-rank test), with the Wasserstein barycenter outperforming the arithmetic mean on up to 16.6\% of held-out annotation divergence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Video summarization is an essential task for content indexing, video retrieval, and automated media consumption. Formally, given a video consisting of $N$ temporal frames or shot segments, the objective is to produce an importance score vector $x \in \mathbb{R}^N$ that selects the most salient and informative moments. 

To train supervised summarization models, benchmark datasets such as TVSum~\cite{song2015tvsum} collect frame-level annotations from multiple independent human raters (typically $K = 20$ annotators per video). However, human perception of importance is intrinsically subjective: one viewer may prioritize narrative dialogues, another action-heavy sequences, and a third specific character interactions. 

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{benchmark_50videos_distribution.png}
    \caption{Distribution of held-out $W_2$ distances and per-video improvements across all 50 videos of the TVSum dataset under our leak-free evaluation protocol.}
    \label{fig:benchmark_distribution}
\end{figure}

Despite this rich diversity, standard summarization models---including recent diffusion-based models~\cite{shang2025video}---collapse these $K$ discrete annotation vectors into a single arithmetic average:
\begin{equation}
    \bar{x} = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^K x^{(k)}.
\end{equation}
In the geometry of human preferences, the arithmetic mean suffers from three severe flaws:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Peak Blurring and Phantom Modes}: If annotator A favors frame $t_1$ and annotator B favors frame $t_2$, their Euclidean average creates an artificial, diffuse plateau centered at $(t_1 + t_2)/2$---a point that neither annotator marked as interesting.
    \item \textbf{Simplex Departure}: When individual annotator ratings are normalized into probability distributions over temporal frames ($x^{(k)} \in \Delta^{N-1}$), standard Gaussian diffusion processes add unconstrained noise $x_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t}x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar{\alpha}_t}\epsilon$ with $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$, pushing states into negative coordinates and invalidating probability mass conservation.
    \item \textbf{Metric Misalignment in Loss Functions}: Pointwise mean squared error (MSE) penalizes temporal misalignments identically whether a predicted peak is 1 frame away or 500 frames away, ignoring the underlying temporal transport cost.
\end{enumerate}

To resolve these challenges, this paper presents a unified geometric approach that models multi-annotator summarization directly on the probability simplex $\Delta^{N-1}$ using 1D Optimal Transport (OT) and Dirichlet diffusion.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Diffusion Models for Video Summarization}
Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM)~\cite{ho2020denoising} and continuous score-based generative models~\cite{song2021score} have achieved remarkable success in generation and structured prediction. Recently, Shang et al.~\cite{shang2025video} applied diffusion models to video summarization by framing importance scoring as a conditional reverse diffusion process. However, their formulation relies on Gaussian noise perturbations and targets the arithmetic mean of annotator scores, leaving the intrinsic simplex geometry and multimodal consensus unaddressed.

\subsection{Wasserstein Barycenters and 1D Optimal Transport}
The Wasserstein-2 distance between two probability distributions $p, q \in \Delta^{N-1}$ on a 1D domain $\{1, \dots, N\}$ equipped with quadratic ground metric $c(i, j) = (i - j)^2$ possesses a celebrated closed-form quantile representation~\cite{peyre2019computational,agueh2011barycenters}:
\begin{equation}
    W_2^2(p, q) = \int_0^1 \left( F_p^{-1}(u) - F_q^{-1}(u) \right)^2 du,
\end{equation}
where $F_p(x) = \sum_{i \le x} p_i$ is the cumulative distribution function (CDF) and $F_p^{-1}(u) = \inf \{ x : F_p(x) \ge u \}$ is the generalized quantile function. The Wasserstein barycenter $x^*$ of $K$ distributions $\{x^{(k)}\}_{k=1}^K$ is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    x^* = \arg\min_{x \in \Delta^{N-1}} \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^K W_2^2(x, x^{(k)}).
\end{equation}
In 1D, the quantile function of the barycenter is precisely the arithmetic mean of individual quantile functions:
\begin{equation}
    F_{x^*}^{-1}(u) = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^K F_{x^{(k)}}^{-1}(u).
\end{equation}
This property enables exact, non-iterative barycenter computation without requiring entropic Sinkhorn regularization~\cite{cuturi2014fast}.

\subsection{Diffusion on Manifolds and Simplex Spaces}
Riemannian diffusion models~\cite{de2022riemannian} extend generative modeling to compact manifolds and constrained geometric spaces. For probability distributions, the Dirichlet distribution $\text{Dir}(\alpha)$ provides a natural generative prior on $\Delta^{N-1}$. By modulating the concentration parameter over diffusion time $t \in [0, T]$, diffusion trajectories remain intrinsically non-negative and normalized.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Simplex Formulation of Video Summarization}
Let a video have $N$ frames. Each annotator $k \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ provides raw scores $s^{(k)} \in [1, 5]^N$. We map raw ratings onto the probability simplex $\Delta^{N-1} = \{ x \in \mathbb{R}^N : x_i \ge 0, \sum_{i=1}^N x_i = 1 \}$ using a temperature-scaled softmax transform:
\begin{equation}
    x_i^{(k)} = \frac{\exp(s_i^{(k)} / \tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^N \exp(s_j^{(k)} / \tau)},
\end{equation}
where $\tau > 0$ controls the sharpness of annotator peak preferences.

\subsection{Exact 1D Wasserstein Barycenter Consensus ($x^*$)}
Given training annotators $\{x^{(k)}\}_{k=1}^{K_{\text{train}}}$, we evaluate the inverse CDF on a uniform grid $u \in \{u_m\}_{m=1}^M \subset [0, 1]$:
\begin{equation}
    F_{x^*}^{-1}(u_m) = \frac{1}{K_{\text{train}}} \sum_{k=1}^{K_{\text{train}}} \text{searchsorted}\left( F_{x^{(k)}}, u_m \right).
\end{equation}
The continuous inverse CDF is mapped back to the discrete histogram $x^* \in \Delta^{N-1}$ by accumulating sample counts and normalizing:
\begin{equation}
    x_i^* = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M \mathbf{1}\left( \lfloor F_{x^*}^{-1}(u_m) \rceil = i \right).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Simplex-Preserving Forward Process}
At diffusion step $t \in \{1, \dots, T\}$, the noisy distribution $x_t$ is constructed using a combination of the cosine noise schedule $\bar{\alpha}_t$ and Dirichlet perturbations:
\begin{equation}
    \delta_t \sim \text{Dir}(\mathbf{1}_N), \quad \tilde{x}_t = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_t} x^* + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_t} \delta_t,
\end{equation}
followed by the simplex projection $\text{Proj}_\Delta(\tilde{x}_t)$:
\begin{equation}
    x_t = \text{Proj}_\Delta(\tilde{x}_t) = \frac{\max(\tilde{x}_t, \epsilon)}{\sum_{i=1}^N \max(\tilde{x}_{t, i}, \epsilon)}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Training Objective: Cramér-Regularized Loss and Analytic Gradient}
The network $\hat{x}_0 = f_\theta(x_t, t)$ is parameterized to directly predict the clean target $x_0 = x^*$. To regularize predictions toward the annotator distribution while maintaining exact differentiability, we formulate the total loss as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}(\theta) = \mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}}(\hat{x}_0, x^*) + \lambda \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{Cramér}}(\hat{x}_0, \{x^{(k)}\}),
\end{equation}
where the discrete Cramér distance is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{Cramér}}(\hat{x}_0, \{x^{(k)}\}) = \frac{1}{K_{\text{train}}} \sum_{k=1}^{K_{\text{train}}} \sum_{i=1}^N \left( F_{\hat{x}_0}(i) - F_{x^{(k)}}(i) \right)^2.
\end{equation}
Unlike the quantile-based $W_2$ distance whose gradient involves non-differentiable step functions, the Cramér distance admits an \textbf{exact closed-form analytic gradient}:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{Cramér}}}{\partial \hat{x}_{0, j}} = \frac{2}{K_{\text{train}}} \sum_{k=1}^{K_{\text{train}}} \sum_{i=j}^N \left( F_{\hat{x}_0}(i) - F_{x^{(k)}}(i) \right).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Reverse Diffusion Sampling}
During inference, given pure noise $x_T \sim \text{Dir}(\mathbf{1}_N)$, the reverse diffusion trajectory iterates for $t = T, \dots, 1$:
\begin{align}
    \hat{x}_0 &= f_\theta(x_t, t), \\
    x_{t-1} &= \begin{cases}
        \text{Proj}_\Delta\left( \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}\hat{x}_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}\delta_{t-1} \right) & \text{if } t > 1, \\
        \hat{x}_0 & \text{if } t = 1.
    \end{cases}
\end{align}

\subsection{Leak-Free Evaluation Protocol}
To prevent data leakage, we enforce:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Strict Split}: For each video, 15 annotators form $S_{\text{train}}$ (used for $x^*$ and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Cramér}}$), and 5 annotators form $S_{\text{heldout}}$ (never seen during training).
    \item \textbf{Generated Sample Evaluation}: We run the full reverse diffusion chain to sample $x_{\text{gen}}$ and compute:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{Score} = \frac{1}{|S_{\text{heldout}}|} \sum_{a \in S_{\text{heldout}}} W_2^2(x_{\text{gen}}, a).
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

\section{Discussion and Future Directions}
\label{sec:discussion}
This work establishes the mathematical validity and evaluation integrity of distribution-aware diffusion on the probability simplex. Key future extensions include:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Replacing the lightweight prototype denoiser with a temporal Transformer backbone conditioned on multi-modal video features (CLIP / Video-LLaVA).
    \item Extending evaluation to SumMe and FPVSum benchmarks.
    \item Formulating 2D/multi-dimensional spatial-temporal Optimal Transport for video highlight detection.
\end{enumerate}
